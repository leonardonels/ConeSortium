The ConeSortium is a joke of a repo around four real ROS 2 nodes, written for Formula Student driverless racing.

A student-built car with nobody in it laps a track marked only by cones. Blue on its left, yellow on its right. The track is whatever lies between them.

The cones are cheap, plastic and stationary. They do not drift. They are, by the file's own admission, the most dependable part of the entire pipeline.

  1. 01 RushFinds cones in the LiDAR cloud.
  2. 02 FusedMaps them, then localises on them.
  3. 03 StellationGrows the centerline.
  4. — PoserRetired. One matrix multiply.

Case #: CS-004 / 0Status: AT LARGE
Subject: cuCONERUSHCodename: SPRINTER
EXHIBIT 1

Point cloud in, cone markers out. XYZ filter, RANSAC ground segmentation, voxel clustering and cone-size filtering, all without leaving the GPU.

Measured at 1.1 ms on an RTX 4060 Mobile, 10 ms on an Orin Nano Super. CPU assistance requested on no occasion whatsoever.

Known hazard: if maxHeight sits below the ground plane at full range, far cones are silently dropped and everyone blames the clustering.

Case #: CS-004 / 1github / cuda_cone_rush
Subject: cuCONEFUSEDCodename: CARTOGRAPHER
EXHIBIT 2

EKF-SLAM. Lap 1 builds the cone map with the pose gain frozen. From lap 2 the map is rigid and only the 3×3 pose block updates.

Covariance stays on the device for the whole run. A full-map update drops from 15 ms on a CPU core to 1.3 ms on an Orin Nano.

Primary target: the expensive sensor's self-confidence. The sensor is not informed.

Case #: CS-004 / 2github / cuda_cone_fused
Subject: cuCONESTELLATIONCodename: PATHFINDER
EXHIBIT 3

Triangulates the cone map and grows a centerline through it. Four backends. Outputs compared: byte-identical.

Only measurable difference between them: how hot the Orin got. Subject refers to its output as the Way.
